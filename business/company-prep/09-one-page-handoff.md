## What RedByte Is

RedByte is a browser-based educational IDE for digital logic and FPGA development. Users visually design circuits, simulate and verify their behavior, map inputs and outputs to a real hardware board, and export complete working project files — ready to open in AMD Vivado and synthesize onto a physical chip.

It runs entirely in the browser. No installation. No accounts required for basic use. It is live today at [redbyteapps.dev](https://redbyteapps.dev).

---

## Why It Exists

The founder took a digital logic course in college and found that the gap between learning the theory and understanding the hardware was larger than it needed to be. Textbooks don't interact. Professional EDA tools aren't designed for learners. Simulators don't connect to real hardware workflows.

RedByte is the tool that should have existed for that course — visual enough to make concepts click, real enough to connect to actual hardware.

## What the Company Plans to Sell

RedByte will operate a free + licensed model:

**Free version** — full access to circuit design, simulation, verification, and hardware export. Available to anyone. This is not a trial — it is the baseline product, and it is intentionally useful. A student can use it without ever paying.

**Classroom / Lab license** — for universities, ECE departments, and training programs that want to formally integrate RedByte into their digital logic curriculum. Includes structured lab fixtures, student submission tooling, instructor quickstart materials, and support. Priced for academic department budgets.

**Future Pro tier** — for serious individual learners outside of an institutional context. Not the near-term focus.

---

## Why the Free Version Exists

The mission is educational. Paywalling students out of a learning tool contradicts the reason the product was built. The free version is how RedByte reaches the people it was built for. The institutional license is how the company sustains itself — schools and departments have budgets for curriculum tools; individual students generally don't.

## Where the Company Is Today

The product is working and deployed. The core student workflow — design, verify, hardware map, export, synthesize in Vivado — is functional for combinational circuits and rising-edge single-clock sequential circuits. The codebase has 220 automated tests, a documented architecture, and a full product manual.

The product needs more polish and a few more features before institutional licensing conversations make sense. The company is not yet generating revenue. Formation is the right move now because the software exists, the IP needs a legal home, and the business direction is clear.
