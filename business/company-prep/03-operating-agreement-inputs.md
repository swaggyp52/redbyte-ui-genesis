# Operating Agreement — Founder Decision Memo

**For:** Connor Angiel
**Prepared:** 2026-04-02
**Purpose:** Capture the founder's decisions and open questions that will feed into the LLC Operating Agreement. This is not the legal agreement itself.

> ⚠️ New York LLC law requires a written operating agreement within 90 days of formation. This memo is input for an attorney to draft that agreement. Do not use this memo as a substitute for a real operating agreement.

---

## 1. Ownership

**Decision:** RedByte LLC is a single-member LLC. Connor Angiel holds 100% of the membership interest.

**Capital contribution at formation:** To be determined. This can be nominal (e.g., $100) or can include a valuation of IP being contributed. Ask your attorney what is standard and appropriate for a software business at this stage.

---

## 2. Management

**Decision:** Member-managed. Connor Angiel, as the sole member, has full authority to manage the company. There is no separate manager.

**Practical meaning:** You sign contracts, open accounts, make business decisions, and operate the company without needing approval from anyone else (other than standard legal/tax requirements).

---

## 3. Authority to Sign Contracts

**Decision:** The sole member (Connor Angiel) has full authority to enter into agreements on behalf of the LLC.

**Note for later:** If you eventually hire someone or bring in a business partner, you will want to revisit what signing authority that person has. For now, sole authority is correct.

---

## 4. Intellectual Property

**Key decision:** All intellectual property created in connection with RedByte — including the codebase, design system, documentation, brand assets, export pipeline, and any derivative works — should be owned by the LLC, not by Connor Angiel personally.

**How this works in practice:** At or shortly after formation, the founder should formally assign or license the existing IP to the LLC. An attorney can prepare a simple IP assignment agreement for this.

**Current IP situation:**
- The RedByte codebase is currently authored by Connor Angiel personally.
- The LICENSE file in the repo reads "RedByte Proprietary License v1.0 — No redistribution, no commercial use, no hosting, no derivative competitors. All rights reserved." Copyright holder is listed as Connor Angiel.
- After LLC formation, copyright and license authority should transfer to RedByte LLC.

⚠️ This is a legal step that needs attorney involvement. Do not skip it.

---

## 5. Profit Distributions

**Decision:** As a single-member LLC, profits can be distributed to the sole member at any time at the member's discretion.

**Tax note:** For a single-member LLC taxed as a disregarded entity (the IRS default), income passes through to your personal tax return. Distributions are not treated as salary. Ask your accountant how to handle this practically — particularly when you start receiving revenue.

---

## 6. Adding Future Members

**Decision (current):** The LLC is single-member. There is no plan to add members at this time.

**Future consideration:** If RedByte takes on a co-founder, business partner, or outside investor, the operating agreement will need to be amended and the ownership structure will change significantly. This is a major decision and should involve an attorney when the time comes.

**Recommendation for the initial agreement:** Include a standard clause that allows the operating agreement to be amended by written consent of the members. This makes future amendments straightforward without requiring a full re-draft.

---

## 7. Dissolution and Exit

**Current position:** No specific dissolution plan needed at this stage. Standard language is sufficient.

**Standard dissolution triggers to include:**
- Written decision by the sole member to dissolve
- Any legally required dissolution event under NY LLC law

**Ask your attorney:** What is the standard dissolution language for a single-member LLC in New York?

---

**Decision:** New York state law governs the operating agreement.
